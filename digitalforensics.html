TARVIKA learning area · Evidence + integrity⌬

Digital Forensics

Learn the careful process of preserving, examining and reporting digital evidence. Integrity, authorization, documentation and legal context matter as much as technical tools.

Register your interest ↗All programs
What this field explores

Evidence is only useful when its handling can be trusted.

Digital forensics involves identifying potential digital evidence, preserving it, examining it methodically and communicating observations in a way others can understand and scrutinize.

A learning environment must use devices, disk images and datasets that are owned, supplied for training or explicitly authorized. A classroom exercise is not a legal investigation, and course participation does not qualify someone to provide expert testimony.

Core lensEvidence preservation, examination and reporting
Learning modeControlled datasets, method notes and case-style exercises
Responsible focusAuthorization, privacy, integrity and jurisdictional law
Possible areas of study

Follow a documented and repeatable method.

Topics and tools depend on the specific offering and authorized learning resources.

01 / PRINCIPLES

Forensic process and scope

Define the question, authority, permitted sources, boundaries and documentation requirements.

02 / PRESERVATION

Evidence integrity

Understand write protection concepts, verification checks and why original evidence must be safeguarded.

03 / DOCUMENTATION

Chain of custody

Record who handled an item, when, why and how it was transferred or stored.

04 / SOURCES

Filesystems and metadata

Explore storage structures, timestamps, metadata and their interpretation limits.

05 / EXAMINATION

Structured analysis

Use approved forensic images or sample datasets to search for relevant artifacts methodically.

06 / REPORTING

Findings and limitations

Separate observations from inference, cite methods and explain uncertainty in neutral language.

Research and project ideas

Practice on evidence created for learning.

All examples require a controlled lab image or explicit written permission.

PROJECT IDEA 01

Artifact timeline exercise

Build a timeline from a prepared training image and document the data sources and assumptions.

PROJECT IDEA 02

Integrity and custody log

Create a reproducible evidence-handling record for a simulated case and identify process gaps.

PROJECT IDEA 03

Neutral technical report

Translate a bounded lab examination into a clear report that distinguishes facts from interpretation.

A possible learner roadmap

Protect first. Examine second. Report carefully.

01 / AUTHORIZE

Confirm scope

Verify ownership or written authorization, purpose and lawful boundaries.

02 / PRESERVE

Protect the source

Use the prescribed lab process and record integrity checks and custody.

03 / EXAMINE

Work methodically

Use suitable tools on approved copies and record steps, findings and anomalies.

04 / REPORT

Explain without overclaiming

Document evidence, method, limitations, uncertainty and conclusions carefully.

Who may find it useful

Learners interested in digital evidence, incident response, security operations, compliance or investigative methods.

What to bring

Patience, attention to detail, respect for privacy and a willingness to follow repeatable procedures.

What you may develop

Depending on the course: documentation habits, evidence concepts and a report from a controlled learning exercise.

What is not implied

Legal authority, investigative appointment, court qualification, job, placement, certification or degree. TARVIKA does not award university degrees.

Never examine someone else's device, account or data without clear legal authority or explicit written permission. Do not submit real personal or confidential evidence to a learning portal.

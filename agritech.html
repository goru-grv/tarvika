Agri Tech

Explore how digital tools and evidence can support decisions across agriculture and food systems—starting with the needs, knowledge and constraints of the people who work in them.

Register your interest ↗All programs
What this field explores

Technology should respond to the realities of a field.

Agri Tech brings agriculture and technology into conversation. Depending on the question, this may involve sensing, data, connectivity, software, decision support or new ways to share information.

Good project framing starts by understanding the local context: crops, seasons, connectivity, cost, maintenance, existing practice and who will use the result. A technical prototype alone does not establish agricultural benefit. TARVIKA's course scope, field access and activities are confirmed for each offering.

Core lensAgriculture, field context and useful information
Learning modeProblem framing, data exploration and applied project work
Responsible focusFarmer agency, data consent, feasibility and sustainability
Possible areas of study

Understand the system before designing the tool.

Topics vary by course delivery, field context and available resources.

01 / CONTEXT

Farming and food-system challenges

Map a problem with local stakeholders and consider seasonal, economic, environmental and operational context.

02 / SENSING

Sensors and connected devices

Explore what field sensors can measure, how they are calibrated and what reliability or connectivity limits apply.

03 / FIELD DATA

Collection and quality

Organize observations, track provenance, identify missing data and document how measurements were gathered.

04 / DECISIONS

Data-informed decisions

Examine whether an indicator can support a practical decision and how uncertainty should be communicated.

05 / DESIGN

Accessible digital tools

Consider language, device access, affordability, usability, maintenance and how technology fits current workflows.

06 / IMPACT

Sustainability and evaluation

Define appropriate evaluation questions without assuming that adoption or improved yield is guaranteed.

Research and project ideas

Investigate a local question before scaling a solution.

Projects require permission and suitable partners before collecting field data or testing technology.

PROJECT IDEA 01

Field-data quality study

Compare a small, consented observation set with sensor readings and record conditions and limitations.

PROJECT IDEA 02

Water-use information prototype

Explore what information could help a defined irrigation decision; test usability without claiming water savings.

PROJECT IDEA 03

Farmer-centred service map

Document an information journey with participants' permission and identify accessibility or process gaps.

A possible learner roadmap

Move from field question to evaluated idea.

Any real-world study needs appropriate permissions, participation and practical safeguards.

01 / LISTEN

Understand the context

Define whose question it is and what existing practices already do.

02 / FRAME

Choose a bounded problem

Set a realistic scope, intended decision and measurable learning goal.

03 / PROTOTYPE

Test a suitable approach

Use appropriate data or a safe simulation; document conditions and assumptions.

04 / REVIEW

Assess usefulness and limits

Share results with context, feedback, maintenance and uncertainty included.

Fit and expectations

Technology, agriculture and people belong together.

Who may find it useful

Learners curious about agriculture, IoT, data, sustainability or digital tools, including participants from technical and agricultural backgrounds.

What to bring

Openness to learning domain basics, listening carefully and testing assumptions rather than treating technology as a default answer.

What you may develop

Depending on activities: problem-framing skills, basic sensing/data literacy and a documented applied project.

What is not guaranteed

Farm access, yield gains, income improvements, a job, placement, certification or degree. TARVIKA does not award university degrees.

Do not collect identifiable farmer, land or operational data without informed permission. Any field activity, partner involvement and data handling must be agreed before it begins.

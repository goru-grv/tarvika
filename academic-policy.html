Learning standards and learner support

Academic Policy

This policy sets out the learning and participation principles that guide TARVIKA's training and research-oriented programs.

Program details vary. The syllabus, eligibility, schedule, attendance expectations, assessments, and completion requirements are specific to each program and will be communicated before enrolment where they apply.

1.Purpose

The Academic Policy of TARVIKA Research and Technology establishes a framework for providing structured, practical and responsible learning experiences.

Our academic approach focuses on developing conceptual understanding, practical skills, problem-solving abilities, project experience and continuous learning.

2. Academic Philosophy

TARVIKA believes that effective technology education combines knowledge with practice.

  • Strong conceptual foundations
  • Practical exercises
  • Real-world projects
  • Problem-solving activities
  • Research and exploration
  • Documentation and reflection
  • Continuous improvement
  • Industry-relevant technology skills

Completing a course or learning activity is not considered the end of learning. Students are encouraged to continue developing their skills through practice and independent learning.

3.Programs and Learning Areas

TARVIKA may provide academic and skill-development opportunities across technology-related areas, including:

  • AI & Machine Learning
  • Web Development & DevOps
  • Cyber Security
  • Digital Marketing
  • Space Technology
  • Agri Tech
  • Digital Forensics
  • Other emerging technology areas

The availability, structure and content of individual programs may vary.

4.Learning Approach

TARVIKA encourages students to move from understanding concepts to applying them.

Depending on the program, learning activities may include:

  • Conceptual lessons
  • Practical demonstrations
  • Hands-on exercises
  • Assignments
  • Projects
  • Case studies
  • Research activities
  • Technical documentation
  • Presentations
  • Assessments
  • Portfolio development

Projects are an important part of the learning process because they allow students to connect concepts with practical situations.

5. Student Responsibilities

Students participating in TARVIKA academic programs are expected to:

  • Attend scheduled learning sessions where applicable.
  • Participate actively in academic activities.
  • Complete assignments and projects within applicable timelines.
  • Practice the concepts taught during the program.
  • Maintain academic honesty.
  • Respect instructors, mentors and fellow learners.
  • Use technology and learning resources responsibly.
  • Follow applicable program-specific rules.
  • Take responsibility for their own learning progress.

Students are encouraged to ask questions, experiment, learn from mistakes and improve their work.

6.Attendance and Participation

Where a program has attendance requirements, students are expected to attend regularly and participate in scheduled activities.

Attendance requirements may differ depending on the specific program, course or workshop.

Repeated absence or lack of participation may affect a student's ability to complete practical activities or meet program requirements.

Any specific attendance requirement will be communicated as part of the relevant program.

7.Assignments and Projects

Assignments and projects should represent the student's own learning and effort.Students may be required to:

  • Submit work within specified deadlines.
  • Explain their approach and methodology.
  • Demonstrate their practical implementation.
  • Maintain appropriate project documentation.
  • Present or discuss their work where required.
  • Make improvements based on feedback.

Students are encouraged to maintain an organized portfolio of their projects and learning progress.

8.Academic Integrity

TARVIKA expects students to maintain honesty and integrity in academic work.The following may be considered academic misconduct:

  • Plagiarism
  • Copying another student's work.
  • Submitting someone else's project as one's own.
  • Misrepresenting project results.
  • Falsifying data or information.
  • Unauthorized collaboration.
  • Cheating in assessments.
  • Unauthorized use of another person's intellectual property

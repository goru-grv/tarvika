Research collaboration

Bring a question. Build a thoughtful collaboration.

We welcome research ideas and conversations that connect technology, evidence and real-world context. Start by sharing the question, the people involved and what kind of exchange could help move the work forward.

We are open to proposals that fit our developing research and technology focus. Initial fit is discussed case by case.

Collaboration pathways

Choose a format that fits the question.

These are possible starting points, not a promise that every format is currently available.

ACADEMIC

Universities and learning communities

Discuss research conversations, guest sessions, workshops, student projects or knowledge exchange where institutional roles, approvals and resources are clear.

INDUSTRY

Organizations and practitioners

Explore a scoped challenge brief, domain discussion, technical review or responsible pilot where objectives and capacity align.

RESEARCH COMMUNITY

Mentors, researchers and professionals

Share methods, feedback or subject expertise through a defined discussion or review. Participation depends on availability and mutual agreement.

COMMUNITY

Local needs and public value

Work with people close to a challenge to understand needs before proposing technology, and consider whether the approach is appropriate and useful.

INTERNATIONAL EXCHANGE

Cross-border academic dialogue

We are open to exploring virtual exchange and international research dialogue when suitable collaborators, institutional approvals and agreements are in place.

STUDENT AND EARLY-CAREER PROJECTS

Structured learning through inquiry

Discuss a bounded project, learning question or prototype with an appropriate supervisor, scope, timeline and feedback plan.

Listing a possible pathway does not mean TARVIKA currently has a partner, funding, laboratory, placement or international agreement in that area. Any confirmed arrangement will be described transparently.
How a proposal is considered

A clear process from first note to next step.

Submitting a proposal starts a conversation; any research activity begins only after scope and responsibilities are agreed.

01 / SUBMIT

Describe the question

Share the research subject, field, summary, stage, team and collaboration sought.

02 / REVIEW

Check fit and feasibility

The team may consider relevance, clarity, expertise, ethics, permissions, resources and practical scope.

03 / FOLLOW UP

Clarify or discuss changes

Use the researcher portal to see status, respond to questions or provide requested clarifications.

04 / AGREE

Set written next steps

Before work begins, agree roles, timeline, deliverables, data handling, approvals and any costs.

What to include

Help us understand the proposal.

A focused initial proposal is more useful than a large collection of unstructured documents.

RESEARCH QUESTION

What are you trying to understand?

Describe the problem, context, topic, relevant background and why the question matters.

APPROACH AND STAGE

What have you done so far?

Note the current stage, possible methods, tools, evidence or prototype, including uncertainty and limitations.

PEOPLE AND ROLES

Who is involved?

Identify the lead contact, team, organization and mentor or supervisor only where they have agreed to be named.

THE REQUEST

What kind of exchange would help?

Be specific about discussion, review, workshop, project support or another possible collaboration format.

Responsible research

Protect people, information and expectations.

Good collaboration considers safeguards as part of the research design, not as an afterthought.

ETHICS AND APPROVALS

Get the right permissions first.

Research involving people, students, patients, animals, sensitive settings or regulated activities may require review and approval from an appropriate institution or authority. Do not begin such work without required approvals.

PRIVACY AND DATA

Share only what is safe to share.

Do not include confidential, personally identifiable, patient, student, proprietary or security-sensitive data in the initial submission. Use high-level summaries and safe-to-share links only.

INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

Clarify ownership and permitted use.

Background materials, code, datasets, inventions, authorship and publication expectations should be discussed and recorded by the relevant parties before collaborative work proceeds.

SCOPE AND CAPACITY

Agree what can realistically be done.

Activities depend on the question, expertise, availability, resources and approvals. A proposal may need revisions or may not be taken forward.

Common questions

Before you submit.

Does submitting guarantee acceptance or a partnership?

No. A submission is an enquiry for initial consideration only. The team may ask questions, suggest changes, identify a possible conversation or decide that it is not a current fit.

Does TARVIKA provide funding or research grants?

No funding or grant is implied by this page. If a specific funding opportunity exists, its source, eligibility, amount and terms must be stated separately and confirmed in writing.

Can I track the proposal later?

Yes. Submit using your researcher account, then sign in to the researcher portal to view your proposal status, team feedback and follow-up messages.

Does TARVIKA award university degrees?

No. TARVIKA is research- and learning-oriented and does not award university degrees. Any certificate or academic recognition must identify the issuing organization and its terms.

Can I attach a full paper or dataset?

The initial portal accepts a safe-to-share HTTPS link, not file uploads. Do not share sensitive or confidential data. Additional materials can be discussed through an agreed secure process if a proposal moves forward.

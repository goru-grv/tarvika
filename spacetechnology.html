Space Technology

Explore the systems, data and applications that make space activity useful on Earth and beyond, from satellite fundamentals to remote-sensing questions and responsible mission thinking.

Register your interest ↗All programs
What this field explores

Understand the system behind the signal.

Space technology spans spacecraft, launch and ground systems, communications, positioning, observation and data services. A learning project can focus on understanding one part of that system or how its information supports a defined application.

Study depth depends on course design and participant background. Work can use public datasets, simulations or conceptual design rather than access to spacecraft, launch facilities or restricted systems.

Core lensSpace systems, satellites, data and applications
Learning modeSystems study, public data analysis and scoped projects
Responsible focusData interpretation, orbital sustainability and lawful use
Possible areas of study

Connect mission concepts with useful data.

Topics depend on the specific offering and available learning resources.

01 / SYSTEMS

Space mission fundamentals

Explore mission objectives, subsystems, constraints, operations and the roles of ground infrastructure.

02 / SATELLITES

Orbits and satellite functions

Build introductory understanding of orbit types, payload purpose, coverage and revisit considerations.

03 / OBSERVATION

Remote sensing and Earth data

Understand image bands, spatial resolution, metadata and why observation conditions matter.

04 / ANALYSIS

Geospatial data workflows

Work with suitable open datasets to ask bounded questions about land, water, environment or infrastructure.

05 / COMMUNICATIONS

Signals and ground segments

Explore high-level communication links, ground stations and how mission data moves through a system.

06 / STEWARDSHIP

Responsible and sustainable space

Discuss debris, spectrum coordination, data interpretation, safety and peaceful, lawful uses of space.

Research and project ideas

Use a clear question to guide the data.

Example project work may use public, properly attributed datasets or simulations.

PROJECT IDEA 01

Earth-observation comparison

Compare public imagery across time while documenting resolution, season and interpretation limits.

PROJECT IDEA 02

Mission concept study

Frame a notional mission objective and map high-level subsystem needs and operational constraints.

PROJECT IDEA 03

Satellite data application brief

Assess whether a selected public data source can inform a defined environmental or planning question.

A possible learner roadmap

From mission purpose to evidence-aware output.

The actual sequence is tailored to the course format and learner foundations.

01 / DEFINE

State the mission question

Clarify the purpose, user, constraints and kind of information needed.

02 / MAP

Understand the system

Identify relevant satellites, sensors, data sources or system components.

03 / ANALYZE

Work with suitable evidence

Use a public dataset or simulation and document processing choices.

04 / COMMUNICATE

Explain findings and limits

Present results with uncertainty, provenance and responsible-use context.

Fit and expectations

Curiosity about systems and evidence.

Who may find it useful

Learners interested in satellites, engineering concepts, geospatial data, Earth observation or space applications.

What to bring

Interest in systems, careful reading of data and willingness to learn technical foundations step by step.

What you may develop

Depending on course work: systems thinking, introductory geospatial analysis and a documented concept or data project.

What is not guaranteed

Access to a space agency, launch site, satellite hardware, flight experience, job, placement, certification or degree.

Use public datasets according to their license and attribution terms. Never imply that a classroom or notional project is an operational space mission.

Know the signs

What Counts as Ragging?

Ragging is not limited to physical violence. Harmful, coercive or humiliating conduct can take many forms, including in digital spaces.

01

Verbal Harassment

Insulting, humiliating, threatening or verbally abusing a fresher or junior.

02

Psychological Harassment

Actions that create fear, humiliation, anxiety, shame or mental distress.

03

Forced Activities

Pressuring a student to perform acts they would not ordinarily choose to do.

04

Physical Harassment

Physical assault, intimidation or harmful physical activity.

05

Public Humiliation

Forcing students to behave in embarrassing, degrading or humiliating ways.

06

Online / Digital Harassment

Using messages, social media, groups or digital platforms to threaten, humiliate or harass.

These examples are for awareness and are not a new legal definition. The applicable definition and prohibited conduct are governed by the relevant institutional and regulatory framework. Read the UGC student regulations ↗

Clear expectations

Strict Anti-Ragging Guidelines

Every student has a part in keeping learning spaces safe and respectful.

01

No Harassment

Do not verbally, physically, psychologically or digitally harass another student.

!
02

No Forced Activities

Never force a junior or another student to perform an activity against their willingness.

!
03

No Humiliation

Do not insult, shame, threaten or deliberately embarrass another student.

!
04

No Abuse of Seniority

Being a senior does not give anyone authority to control, intimidate or punish juniors.

!
05

No Group Intimidation

Do not take part in group behavior intended to frighten, isolate or pressure another student.

!
06

No Digital Ragging

Ragging and harassment through social media, messaging platforms or online groups are also unacceptable.

!
07

Do Not Encourage Others

Do not support, record, share, encourage or silently facilitate ragging.

!
08

Report What You Witness

Do not treat ragging as entertainment or “tradition”. Report it through an appropriate channel.

!
A better welcome

Being a Senior Is a Responsibility — Not a License.

Guide juniors. Welcome them. Help them understand the environment. Never use seniority as a reason to intimidate, humiliate or control another student.

Instead of ragging
  • Introduce yourself
  • Welcome new students
  • Help them understand campus life
  • Share useful academic information
  • Encourage healthy interaction
  • Build friendships through respect
You deserve support

If You Are Experiencing Ragging

You do not have to manage this alone. Take the steps that feel safe for you.

01

Move to Safety

If you feel threatened or unsafe, move away from the situation and seek a safe environment.

02

Tell Someone You Trust

Speak to a teacher, mentor, parent or guardian, institutional authority or another trusted person.

03

Record Relevant Information

Where safe and appropriate, note the date, time, location, people involved, what happened and relevant messages or other evidence. Do not put yourself at risk to collect evidence.

04

Report It

Use your institution’s official reporting mechanism and/or the National Anti-Ragging Helpline.

Register an official complaint ↗
05

Seek Support

If the incident has affected your mental or physical wellbeing, seek appropriate professional or emergency support.

Bystanders can help

Saw Something? Don’t Stay Silent.

Silence can allow harmful behavior to continue. Reporting can help protect someone.

  • Do not encourage the behavior.
  • Do not participate.
  • Do not share humiliating recordings.
  • Help the affected student reach a safe person.
  • Inform the appropriate authority.
  • Report through official channels.

Rules and due process

Ragging Can Have Serious Consequences

Ragging is prohibited and can result in disciplinary and other action under applicable institutional and regulatory frameworks.

  • Disciplinary proceedings
  • Suspension or other academic restrictions
  • Removal from institutional activities
  • Other penalties under applicable rules
  • Referral to appropriate authorities where required

Consequences depend on the nature of the incident and the applicable rules, regulations and due process. Review the official regulations ↗

Clear answers

Student Safety FAQs

If you need help now, call the National Anti-Ragging Helpline.

1800-180-5522
Is ragging only physical?

No. Ragging can include verbal, psychological, humiliating, coercive and other prohibited conduct depending on the applicable rules.

What if someone says it is “just fun”?

Calling it a joke does not make harmful or coercive behavior acceptable.

Can online harassment be reported?

Yes. Relevant digital or online harassment should be reported through appropriate institutional or official channels.

What should I do if I witness ragging?

Do not participate or encourage it. Help the affected student reach safety and report the incident through an appropriate channel.

What if I am afraid to report?

Speak to a trusted person or contact the official anti-ragging helpline to understand your options.

Where can I file a complaint?

Use your institution’s official reporting mechanism or the National Anti-Ragging complaint portal.

Help is available

If It Doesn’t Feel Right, Speak Up.

You do not have to handle ragging alone. Reach out to someone you trust or use an official reporting channel.

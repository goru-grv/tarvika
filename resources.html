01 · Getting started

Build foundations in a useful order

Roadmap · AI & machine learning
AI and Machine Learning Starter Roadmap
  1. Programming: practise Python basics, functions, files, and debugging with small scripts.
  2. Data: load a permitted dataset, inspect columns and missing values, and write down what each record represents.
  3. Statistics: review averages, variation, probability, and how a sample can differ from a population.
  4. Models: start with a simple baseline before trying a more complex method.
  5. Evaluation: reserve data for evaluation, inspect errors, and state what the score cannot establish.
  6. Project: publish a short report with the question, source, method, result, and limits.

First milestone: Explain one model’s input, output, evaluation method, and two failure cases in your own words.

Learning path · Cybersecurity
Cybersecurity Safe Practice Path
  1. Learn networking, operating-system, identity, and web fundamentals.
  2. Practise only in a local lab, purpose-built training environment, or system where you have written authorization.
  3. Keep a lab notebook: objective, environment, commands, observations, and cleanup.
  4. Focus on defensive habits: updates, access controls, backups, logging, and incident documentation.

Boundary: Never probe public systems, accounts, or networks without explicit authorization and agreed scope.

02 · Project planning

Turn a broad idea into a bounded project

Worksheet · Project scope
Student Project Scope Canvas
  • Question: What do I want to find out or make?
  • Context: Who is affected, and what do I already know?
  • Outcome: What can I demonstrate by the end?
  • Minimum version: What is the smallest useful test?
  • Inputs and permissions: What data, tools, people, approvals, or equipment are needed?
  • Evidence: What observation would answer the question?
  • Limits: What will this project not establish?
  • Next step: What is the first task I can complete in one session?

Write a one-sentence scope: “I will [ACTION] for [PURPOSE] using [METHOD] by [DATE]; I will assess it by [EVIDENCE].”

Checklist · Documentation
Technology Project Documentation Checklist
  • State the question, intended user, and project boundary.
  • Record tools, versions, data sources, licenses, and setup steps.
  • Describe important decisions and alternatives considered.
  • Keep test cases, results, errors, and changes.
  • Separate observed results from interpretation.
  • List known limitations, risks, and unresolved questions.
  • Remove secrets and private data before sharing.
  • Give another person enough information to reproduce the safe parts of the work.
Worksheet · Agri Tech
Agri Tech Problem-Framing Worksheet
  • Describe the agricultural task or decision in the local context.
  • Who currently performs it, and what constraints shape the work?
  • What evidence shows the problem matters?
  • What non-technical options have been considered?
  • Would a proposed tool work with local connectivity, cost, maintenance, language, and climate conditions?
  • How could farmers or affected communities review the idea safely?
  • What small, low-risk test could reveal whether the approach is useful?

Do not treat a prototype or desk study as proof of field effectiveness.

03 · Responsible practice

Ask before collecting, building, or sharing

Checklist · Data use
Responsible Data Use Questions
  • Where did the data come from, and may it be used for this purpose?
  • Does it contain personal, sensitive, confidential, or proprietary information?
  • Could individuals be identified by combining fields?
  • Does the dataset represent the context I am making claims about?
  • How will data be stored, accessed, retained, and deleted?
  • Do the people or institutions involved need to give consent or approval?
  • Can I complete the task with less data or synthetic examples?
  • What will I avoid uploading to third-party tools?

If permissions or safeguards are unclear, pause data collection and ask the relevant institution or data owner.

Checklist · AI projects
Responsible AI Review Questions
  • What task is the system intended to support, and what is out of scope?
  • Who could be affected by an incorrect or uneven result?
  • What baseline or non-AI alternative was considered?
  • How were data quality, representation, and evaluation checked?
  • Can a person review, challenge, or override the output?
  • What information is sent to the tool provider?
  • How will errors be monitored and reported?
  • What evidence would justify stopping or changing the system?
04 · Research practice

Make the question and request clear

Worksheet · Research
Research Proposal Planning Worksheet
  • Working title and question: What are you trying to understand?
  • Context: What is already known, and what remains uncertain?
  • Importance: Who could benefit from an answer?
  • Approach: What methods and evidence may fit the question?
  • Current stage: Idea, literature review, prototype, study, or analysis?
  • Team and roles: Who is involved, and have they agreed to be named?
  • Safeguards: What approvals, consent, data, or IP questions need resolution?
  • Specific request: What conversation, review, or support are you seeking?
  • Feasibility: What timeline, expertise, and resources are required?

Do not include confidential or sensitive data in an initial enquiry.

Starter guide · Earth observation
Satellite Data Exploration Starter Guide
  1. Write an observable question tied to a place and time period.
  2. Identify the measurement needed; do not assume an image directly measures the outcome of interest.
  3. Find a documented, permitted source and read its metadata, resolution, and known limitations.
  4. Check cloud, seasonal, sensor, and geographic coverage before analysis.
  5. Compare observations with suitable ground context where available.
  6. Document processing steps and uncertainty; avoid claims beyond the evidence.

Begin with the NASA Earthdata learning portal: NASA Earthdata Learn.

05 · Career preparation

Present skills with evidence

Checklist · Portfolio
Beginner Portfolio Review Checklist
  • Each project has a clear question and a short summary.
  • Your individual contribution is stated accurately.
  • Tools and methods are described at the level needed to understand the work.
  • Evidence includes a demo, screenshots, results, or a written explanation where appropriate.
  • Limitations and unfinished areas are explicit.
  • Links work and do not expose credentials, private data, or confidential work.
  • Projects are relevant to the roles or learning opportunities you are pursuing.
Worksheet · Resume
Resume Evidence Builder

For each experience, write a factual note before turning it into a resume bullet:

  • Context: What project, course, or responsibility?
  • Action: What did you personally do?
  • Method: What tools or approach did you use?
  • Evidence: What deliverable or verified result can you point to?
  • Scope: What was your role, timeframe, and team context?

Use numbers only when you can explain their source. Do not claim team outcomes as individual results.

Planner · Interview practice
Technology Interview Practice Planner
  1. Choose one target role and note its main responsibilities.
  2. Prepare a two-minute explanation of a project: problem, your role, choices, result, and limitation.
  3. Practise a relevant fundamentals question aloud; explain your reasoning, not only the answer.
  4. Prepare an example of learning from an error or changing your approach.
  5. Write three questions about the team, supervision, expectations, or role terms.
  6. After practice, record one strength and one specific area to improve.
Tool · Learning choices
Program Comparison Worksheet
  • Does the curriculum match my goal and starting level?
  • How much practice, feedback, and project work is included?
  • What are the schedule, duration, format, prerequisites, and full fees?
  • Who provides instruction and learner support?
  • What certificate or recognition is offered, and who issues it?
  • What equipment, software, or data access will I need?
  • What outcomes are supported by evidence, and what is not guaranteed?

Compare written terms from each provider before paying or enrolling.
